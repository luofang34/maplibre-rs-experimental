Module spikes 

Source
Expand description

Removal of the narrow elevation spikes that some DEM mosaics carry along source seams.

Resampling across a seam leaves runs of one or two samples above both neighbours, from tens to hundreds of metres, down north–south columns of the tile. Two rules lower such a sample to the median of its window:

  • A peak rule for the tallest spikes: a sample that rises more than SPIKE_SLOPE ground sample distances above its row or column median.
  • A seam rule for the lower rest of a seam: a sample that rises more than SEAM_FLOOR_SAMPLES ground sample distances, and at least SEAM_FLOOR_METRES, above its east–west median, where such rises continue down the same column for at least SEAM_ROWS rows.

Both rules only lower samples. Coarse tiles, where one sample can hold a real isolated peak, have thresholds of kilometres and keep their peaks. Narrow pits are kept: a gorge one sample wide is real terrain, and a north–south gorge has the same column signature as the seams’ shallow ringing dips, which do not stand out against the horizon.

Constants§

MAX_ENCODED 🔒
Encoded value range of the three 8-bit channels.
SEAM_FLOOR_METRES 🔒
Policy: the smallest rise in metres that can belong to a seam. Fine tiles have samples of a few metres, where steep real ridges reach the sample-based floor; the seams rise far above it.
SEAM_FLOOR_SAMPLES 🔒
Policy: the rise above the east–west median, in ground sample distances, from which a sample can belong to a seam: a 45° climb on both flanks. Lower rises are left to real terrain.
SEAM_ROWS 🔒
Policy: the rows a seam’s rise must run down one column, give or take SEAM_SLACK. A ridge one or two samples wide does not stay within three columns for sixteen rows unless it runs within 7° of due north, while the seams follow one column for a hundred rows or more.
SEAM_SLACK 🔒
Columns either side of a column that continue its seam: ringing wanders by one sample.
SPIKE_SLOPE 🔒
Policy: how many ground sample distances a sample may rise above its window median before it counts as a spike. Seam ringing is one or two samples wide and stands hundreds of metres proud. For terrain to rise three sample distances above the median of its five-sample window, it must climb steeper than atan(3), about 72°, on both flanks. Bare-earth DEMs average the ground over each sample and hold no such slopes, so steep real relief stays untouched.
WINDOW_RADIUS 🔒
Samples on each side of a sample in its row and column windows. Two lets a spike two samples wide stay a minority of its window.
