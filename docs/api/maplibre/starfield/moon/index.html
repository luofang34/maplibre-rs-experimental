Module moon 

Source
Expand description

The moon’s disc, lit on the side the sun direction shows it, and its halo.

The phase is geometry only: each pixel of the disc stands for a point on a sphere, lit where its normal faces the sun direction, so the lit share and the bright limb’s orientation both follow from the sun and moon directions. The surface scatters as the moon’s regolith does, so a full moon is as bright at its limb as at its centre, and saturates towards white well above any star. A halo around it falls with the square of the angle, as bright as the moon’s magnitude at its phase. The dark side keeps a faint earthshine. With no sun direction set the whole disc is drawn lit, a fallback rather than a phase. The disc lies at the far plane like the sun’s, and does not fade with the sky: the moon shows by day.
